01
The Problem

Identifying potholes through road imagery

Potholes can damage vehicles and create road safety hazards. Traditional road inspections require time and manual effort, making it challenging to monitor large road networks efficiently.

This project explored how deep learning could support pothole identification by classifying road images according to whether a pothole is present.

The group also investigated how simulated rainy conditions could be incorporated into model development to reflect changes in road image appearance.

02
Dataset

MIIA Pothole Image Classification Challenge

The project used the MIIA Pothole Image Classification Challenge dataset, containing 5,676 road images.

The task was binary image classification: predicting whether a road image contained a pothole.

1 Pothole Present

04
Model Development

Comparing deep learning approaches

The group compared transfer learning and fine-tuning approaches using ResNet50 and MobileNetV2.

ResNet50 provided an initial benchmark. MobileNetV2 was then explored as a lighter-weight architecture suitable for further fine-tuning.

Model / Approach Test Accuracy
ResNet50 — Transfer Learning 93.47%
MobileNetV2 — Transfer Learning 85.52%
MobileNetV2 — Fine-Tuned 98.85%
MobileNetV2 — Rain-Augmented 96.61%

Fine-tuning MobileNetV2 produced a reported test accuracy of 98.85% on the original-dataset experiment.

The separate rain-augmented MobileNetV2 experiment achieved a reported test accuracy of 96.61%. These results should be interpreted in the context of their respective experimental datasets.

05
Data Augmentation

Simulating rainy road conditions

The original dataset primarily represented clear-weather conditions. To introduce additional image variation, the group explored weather-based data augmentation.

Rain effects were applied using the Automold road augmentation library. The group explored drizzle, heavy rain and torrential rain, as well as snow and fog simulations.

More severe effects obscured road details, making potholes difficult to distinguish. The group therefore selected drizzle for the main rain-augmentation experiment.

06
Explainable AI

Understanding model predictions with Grad-CAM

Accuracy alone does not explain which parts of an image influence a model's classification.

The group applied Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) to generate heatmaps highlighting image regions that contributed to model predictions.

These visualisations helped investigate whether the model focused on potholes or unrelated features in the surrounding scene.

Why explainability matters

Grad-CAM helped the group examine model behaviour beyond accuracy scores and identify examples where background features influenced predictions.

07
Error Analysis

Learning from incorrect classifications

The group examined images associated with high prediction losses to better understand model errors.

Grad-CAM visualisations revealed examples where the model attended to irrelevant scene features, including the sun, roadside buildings and lamps.

This highlighted the importance of testing models against varied lighting, backgrounds and environmental conditions.

Key Learning

A high classification accuracy does not guarantee that a model always relies on the intended visual features.

08
Limitations

From simulated images to real roads

Simulated rainfall can increase image variation, but a rain filter cannot fully reproduce the complexity of real wet-weather road conditions.

Strong augmentation may also obscure the visual information required for correct classification.

Future work could evaluate the models on independently collected rainy road images and investigate additional lighting and environmental conditions.

09
