01
The Problem

Finding the right fragrance

Choosing a perfume can be difficult because fragrances contain multiple scent accords and notes, while personal preferences vary significantly between individuals.

Traditional product browsing often requires users to manually compare fragrances across different brands, scent families, ratings and popularity levels.

This project explores whether natural language processing and machine learning can simplify fragrance discovery by recommending perfumes with similar scent profiles.

02
Dataset

Understanding 131,930 fragrances

The project uses a Fragrantica perfume dataset containing fragrance characteristics, scent accords, notes, ratings and popularity-related information.

03
Exploratory Data Analysis

Exploring fragrance characteristics

Exploratory analysis was performed to understand the distribution of scent accords and investigate how fragrance characteristics relate to customer ratings.

Woody, powdery, sweet, citrus and aromatic accords appeared frequently across the dataset.

Fragrance Accord Count
Woody 81,432

These results helped establish the dominant fragrance characteristics in the dataset before developing the recommendation engine.

04
Rating Analysis

What influences perfume ratings?

A separate analysis investigated relationships between fragrance characteristics and average user ratings.

Longevity and sillage showed positive correlations with fragrance ratings, suggesting that perceived performance is associated with customer evaluations.

Variable Correlation
Longevity 0.2849
Sillage 0.2114
Want 0.1320
Release Year -0.0828

These relationships are associations rather than evidence that any individual characteristic directly causes higher ratings.

05
Machine Learning

Predicting fragrance ratings

A Random Forest regression model was developed to investigate how fragrance attributes contribute to average user ratings.

MEAN ABSOLUTE ERROR 0.1932
R² SCORE 0.1746

The model achieved a mean absolute error of approximately 0.19 rating points, but its R² of 0.175 indicates that much of the variation in ratings remained unexplained.

This highlights the difficulty of predicting subjective customer preferences using fragrance attributes alone.

07
Recommendation System

Combining similarity with popularity

The recommendation engine uses a hybrid scoring approach that combines fragrance similarity, average ratings and vote-based popularity.

70% — Similarity

How closely a fragrance matches the reference perfume's text representation.

20% — Rating

Incorporates the fragrance's average customer rating.

10% — Vote Score

Incorporates rating volume as a popularity-related signal.

This approach balances fragrance resemblance with rating and popularity information rather than relying entirely on textual similarity.

08
Key Takeaways

What I learned

Text data can power product discovery.

TF-IDF and cosine similarity provide a way to recommend products based on descriptive attributes rather than requiring historical user interactions.

Customer ratings are subjective.

The rating prediction model demonstrated that fragrance characteristics alone cannot explain most variation in customer ratings.

Recommendation systems can combine multiple signals.

Incorporating similarity, rating and popularity provides a more flexible approach to ranking candidate fragrances.
