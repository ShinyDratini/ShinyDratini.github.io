01
The Problem

Forecasting retail demand

Retail demand is influenced by more than historical sales. Promotions, store activity, calendar effects and recent demand patterns can all affect future sales.

The objective of this project was to build and progressively improve a machine-learning model capable of forecasting store sales while identifying which operational variables contributed most to predictive performance.

02
Dataset

More than 3 million observations

The project uses the Corporación Favorita grocery sales dataset, containing historical sales across multiple stores and product families.

date Sales date
store_nbr Store identifier

sales Target variable

03
Feature Engineering

Turning historical data into predictive signals

Time-series and operational features were engineered to capture recent demand, longer-term patterns, calendar effects and changes in store activity.

Lag Features

7-day, 14-day and 28-day historical sales.

Rolling Features

7-day and 30-day rolling demand patterns.

Calendar Features

Day of week, month and day of month.

Promotion

Promotional activity affecting product demand.

Holiday Events

Calendar and holiday-related demand effects.

Transactions

Store activity used as an additional demand signal.

04

07
Model Interpretation

What drove the predictions?

Transaction volume became the most influential feature in the final XGBoost model, representing approximately 45.9% of feature importance.

Holiday information, recent rolling sales and day-of-week patterns also contributed to the model, highlighting the importance of combining historical demand with operational and calendar context.

Transactions 45.9%
08
Key Takeaways

From prediction to business insight

Operational activity matters.

Transaction volume provided a powerful signal of underlying store activity and substantially improved forecasting performance.

Feature engineering was critical.

Lag, rolling, calendar, promotion and holiday features allowed the model to capture different dimensions of retail demand.

Model development should be iterative.

Measuring performance after each improvement made it possible to understand which additional data actually created predictive value.
